**IPTV (Internet Protocol Television) is television delivered over an internet connection instead of a cable line, satellite dish or antenna.** You watch live channels, a TV guide and on-demand movies and series through an app on your Smart TV, streaming stick, phone or computer. All you need is a stable internet connection, a compatible device and an IPTV subscription.

This guide explains how IPTV works, how it compares with cable and streaming apps, what equipment and internet speed you need, and what it typically costs in 2026.

## How Does IPTV Work?

IPTV sends TV as data packets over the same internet connection you use for browsing. The process has four steps:

1. **The provider receives the channels** and encodes them into compressed digital streams.
2. **The streams are stored on servers** spread across several data centres, so viewers connect to one near them.
3. **Your app requests a stream** when you pick a channel or title. The server sends it to your device over the internet.
4. **Your device decodes and plays it** in SD, HD, Full HD or 4K, depending on the stream and your connection.

Because the stream is delivered on request, you only download the channel you are watching. That is why IPTV can offer thousands of channels without a dish or a cable box.

## IPTV vs Cable, Satellite and Streaming Apps

| | IPTV | Cable / satellite | Streaming apps |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Delivery** | Internet connection | Cable line or dish | Internet connection |
| **Live channels** | Hundreds to thousands | Usually 100 to 500 | None or a few |
| **On-demand library** | Usually included | Limited, often extra | The main product |
| **Equipment** | Any compatible device | Provider's box, installation | Any compatible device |
| **Contract** | Usually none | Often 12 to 24 months | None |
| **Typical monthly cost** | About $5 to $20 | About $80 to $150 | About $8 to $23 per app |

The main difference from streaming apps is **live TV**. Streaming apps mostly offer their own on-demand catalogue, while IPTV focuses on live channels plus a large on-demand library.

## How Do You Log In to an IPTV Service?

After you subscribe, the provider sends your login details in one of three formats:

- **Xtream Codes:** a username, password and server URL. This is the most common and easiest format, and it loads channels, the TV guide and on-demand content automatically.
- **M3U playlist:** a single link to a channel list. Usually paired with a separate EPG link for the TV guide.
- **MAG portal:** a portal URL linked to your box's MAC address.

You enter these details once in your player app, and it remembers them.

## Is IPTV Legal?

IPTV is a delivery technology, and the technology itself is legal. Many licensed TV operators use IPTV to deliver their own services. Whether a specific IPTV service is legal depends on whether it holds the rights to the content it distributes, and on the laws where you live. Check the provider's terms, and the rules in your country, before subscribing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does IPTV stand for?**
IPTV stands for Internet Protocol Television: TV delivered over an internet (IP) network instead of cable, satellite or an antenna.

**Do I need a special box for IPTV?**
No. Most Smart TVs, streaming sticks, phones and computers can run an IPTV app. A set-top box such as a MAG is optional.

**Can I watch IPTV on more than one TV?**
Yes, if your plan includes multiple connections. Each screen that watches at the same time usually needs its own connection.

**Why does IPTV buffer?**
Buffering usually comes from a slow or unstable internet connection, a weak Wi-Fi signal, or an overloaded server. Use a wired connection, close other downloads, and choose a provider with load-balanced servers.

**What is the difference between IPTV and OTT?**
OTT (over-the-top) is a broad term for any video delivered over the internet, including streaming apps. IPTV usually refers to live TV channels delivered over the internet with a TV guide, as a cable replacement.
